# 7-Day Healthy Vegetarian South Indian Meal Plan Prompt

## FOR — Editable User Details

* Age: 53
* Activity level: Moderate
* Goal: Gradual and sustainable weight loss
* Dietary preference: Vegetarian
* Location: South India
* Allergies: None
* Foods I dislike: None
* Foods I prefer: South Indian home-style food
* Weekday cooking time: 30–45 minutes
* Number of people eating: 1
* Other dietary restrictions: None

## ROLE

Act as a qualified nutrition-focused meal-planning assistant with good knowledge of traditional South Indian vegetarian cuisine. Create practical, balanced and realistic meal plans using commonly available ingredients in South India.

This is a general meal-planning exercise, not medical or personalised dietary advice. Do not diagnose medical conditions or prescribe restrictive diets.

## TASK

Create a healthy **7-day vegetarian South Indian meal plan** designed to support gradual and sustainable weight loss.

The plan must include exactly four meals for every day:

1. Breakfast
2. Lunch
3. Snack
4. Dinner

Use real, recognisable South Indian dishes rather than generic descriptions such as "healthy breakfast" or "vegetable meal."

Examples of suitable foods include idli, dosa, pongal, upma, sambar, rasam, vegetable kootu, poriyal, curd rice, vegetable rice, chapati, sundal, buttermilk and other appropriate South Indian vegetarian dishes.

## CONSTRAINTS

* Keep the plan vegetarian.
* Keep the food recognisably South Indian and suitable for a typical South Indian household.
* Prefer ordinary, locally available and affordable ingredients.
* Use commonly available vegetables, lentils, pulses, rice, millets, curd, vegetables and fruits.
* Include a reasonable balance of carbohydrates, protein, vegetables, fibre and healthy fats across the day.
* Give sensible household portion sizes rather than extreme calorie restriction.
* Support gradual weight loss without recommending starvation, meal skipping or crash diets.
* Avoid very large portions and excessive use of oil, sugar, salt and fried foods.
* Include protein sources such as dal, sambar, kootu, sundal, sprouts, curd, paneer or other suitable vegetarian foods where appropriate.
* Include vegetables regularly throughout the week.
* Include fruit where appropriate.
* Keep weekday preparation practical and reasonably quick.
* Do not build the plan around foods that are not typical or easily available locally.
* Do not make quinoa, avocado, imported superfoods, protein powders or similar foods the foundation of the plan.
* Avoid repeating the exact same full meal every day.
* Allow occasional traditional foods in sensible portions.
* Do not recommend supplements or medication for weight loss.
* If an allergy, dislike or dietary restriction is listed in the FOR section, completely avoid that ingredient or food.
* If important information is missing, make a reasonable assumption and clearly state it rather than inventing medical requirements.

## PORTION GUIDELINES

Use practical household measurements such as:

* Number of idlis/dosas
* Cups of cooked rice
* Number of chapatis
* Cups or bowls of sambar/kootu/vegetables
* Number of fruits
* Small handfuls for nuts or sundal
* Glasses or cups for buttermilk/curd

Keep portions moderate and realistic for the person's activity level.

Do not prescribe dangerously low calorie intake.
